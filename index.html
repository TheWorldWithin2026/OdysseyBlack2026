Executive Domination
Odyssey Black War Room
The strategic playbook to become Atlanta's #1 black car service for Fortune 500 executives.
01
Own the Routes, Not the Rides
The play: Every Fortune 500 executive flying ATL-NYC on Monday morning is a standing reservation waiting to happen. Your goal is not to get booked once — it is to own their departure slot permanently.

How: When a prospect has a predictable travel pattern (Mon/Thu, ATL-NYC), propose a standing reservation by name. "I want to own your Monday 6AM to LaGuardia." That specificity closes deals that generic pitches cannot.

Revenue math: One executive, ATL-NYC weekly = $3,200/month. Ten executives on standing routes = $32,000 MRR. This is the recurring contract model that separates you from every rideshare and one-off car service in Atlanta.
02
The EA Network Is Your Sales Force
The play: Executive assistants control access to every C-suite calendar in Atlanta. Most car services ignore them. You will treat them like the VIP decision-makers they are.

How: Build a relationship with every EA in your pipeline before you ever contact their executive. Send them value first — a relevant industry article, a genuine compliment on how well they manage a complex schedule. Then make the pitch: "I want to make your job easier." An EA who trusts you will put you on the standing travel list without the executive ever receiving a cold pitch.

The multiplier: One EA at Delta Air Lines manages 3-5 C-suite executives. One relationship = five clients.
03
Event Windows Are Revenue Windows
The play: Every major Atlanta business event — ATL CEO Summit, Buckhead Business Association, Georgia 500 conference, Atlanta Business Chronicle events — concentrates your ideal clients in one place at one time with elevated transportation needs.

How: Use Event Mode in the app. Identify every speaker and VIP attendee 2-3 weeks before the event. Score them all. Send event-specific outreach: "I'll be handling executive transportation for several attendees at the ATL CEO Summit next Thursday. I'd like to take care of you as well." The transportation need is real and immediate — you are solving a problem they have right now.

The compounding effect: Executives talk to each other at events. One satisfied client at a conference becomes a referral source for every executive in the room who asks "who was your driver?"
04
The Trial Ride Is the Product Demo
The play: No executive will commit to a recurring service they have never experienced. Remove every barrier to that first ride. Make it impossible to say no.

How: Offer a complimentary or deeply discounted first ride framed as a professional courtesy — not a promotion. "I would like to take care of your next airport departure as my introduction. No obligation." Confirm 24 hours before with a personal message. Send driver information and a photo the morning of. Follow up within 48 hours with a genuine question about the experience.

The close rate: An executive who experiences the service converts at 60-70%. The trial ride is not a cost — it is your highest-ROI marketing investment.
05
Corporate Account Expansion
The play: One executive at a Fortune 500 company is the door. The entire C-suite is the prize. Once you have one active client at Delta, UPS, or Coca-Cola, you have social proof inside that organization.

How: After 30 days with any new client, ask directly: "Who else on your leadership team travels frequently? I would like to extend the same service." Most executives will name 2-3 colleagues without hesitation. Use the Company Intel tab to track every executive at that company and build a coordinated account strategy.

The endgame: A corporate account agreement with a Fortune 500 company — where OD Black is the designated ground transportation provider for all executive travel — is worth $15,000-50,000 per month in recurring revenue.

Data & Backup
How your data is stored

No login for the app. Anyone who opens the site address sees the screens, but the screens hold no data. Your records are saved inside this browser, on this device.

Cloud Sync is separate and optional. It is a sign-in to a Supabase account. Synced rows are stored per signed-in user, and the project is set up so each user can read and change only their own rows (row-level security, defined in supabase/001_ob_store.sql in the repository). Whether that setup is applied on the live project, and whether public sign-ups are turned off, cannot be checked from this app. Confirm both in Supabase.

Companies, contacts, opportunities, quotes, bookings and activity are not synced. They stay in this browser and are included only in Export Full Backup. Keep a backup. A second browser or device would hold different records.

Costs and profit are not stored in the app.

What Full Backup contains
Cloud Sync
Sign in to keep a copy of your data in the cloud so it survives a cleared browser or a new device. The app still works without it.
Export
Restore from a backup file
Restore is not the same as Import. Restore puts back (Replace) or combines (Merge) data from a backup file you downloaded earlier. Import, on the Companies tab, copies your existing Pipeline, Gatekeeper, Research and Radar records into Companies without using a file. Choosing a file below only previews it and changes nothing until you press Restore.
Safe way to test a restore on the live site
  1. In your working browser, note the Companies fingerprint and counts above, then press Export Full Backup. Save the file as your original and do not edit it.
  2. On the Companies tab, press Check import (changes nothing). It reports what an import would add.
  3. Make a copy of the backup file. Use the copy for the test.
  4. Open the live site in a separate browser profile or a private window (it starts empty). Do not sign in to Cloud Sync there.
  5. Choose the copy under Choose Backup File and read the preview. Press Restore (Replace), let the page reload, and open Data & Backup. The fingerprint and counts must equal step 1. Open a company and check its contacts, quote, booking and Pipeline links.
  6. Only if every check matches, trust the backup. Never restore into the working browser unless you need to, and export a fresh backup first. A snapshot is taken automatically before each restore.
